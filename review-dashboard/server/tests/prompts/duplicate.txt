# Add bookkeeping policy

## Before adding

Search the full history for this payee or kind of payment, including entries saved
after the draft. Follow the account names, descriptions, posting structure and notes
used for similar payments. Never invent an amount, date, item, account or exchange rate.

## Duplicates

Check nearby entries for the same payment, using amount, date, similar payees and
any printed reference. If it may already be recorded, ask before proposing another
entry. Never record one payment twice.

## Evidence and dates

Use the calendar date on the receipt or payment. For an undated entry or words
like "yesterday" or "last Friday", use the draft's original Saved time in its
recorded zone, even on a retry. Name attachments in the ledger's usual style,
such as Fable-Receipt. The server files and cites them.

## Double entry

Use exact amounts and balance the entry. Debit expenses (positive) and credit
the account that actually paid (negative). Credit income. Use the accounts that
really moved, even when the payment was intended to come from somewhere else.
Keep payments on the same day in the order they happened.

## Accounts

Use declared accounts and match the person's words to labels and aliases. Ask
when more than one account matches. If a new account is needed, follow the
existing structure and declare it in `new-accounts`.

## Currencies and conversions

Keep totals separate by currency. For a foreign expense, keep the original
amount and commodity on the expense posting and record the amount charged as
its total `cost`, in the paying account's currency. Credit the paying account
with the charged amount. Use `original-amount` only for a foreign conversion.
For a cross-currency transfer, keep the sent and received amounts in their
respective currencies, with a total cost on the sending posting.

When neither the charged or received amount nor the rate is known, ask for one.
If the accounts are declared and all other details are settled, return a question
with two postings. Keep the known positive expense or negative sent amount,
and put `0.00` in the charged or receiving posting's currency. Neither posting
has a cost until the owner answers. Use this marker only for the currency question.

## Transfers

Moving money between the person's own accounts, including paying a card bill,
debits the receiving account and credits the sending account.

## Prepaid wallets

Loading a wallet is a transfer from the account that paid. Spending credits the
wallet and a refund to it debits the wallet.

## People

Paying for someone else debits their person account. A repayment credits that
account, with no income. Only the owner's share of a shared cost is an expense.

## Investments

Debit the investment account with the confirmed units in the holding's commodity
at their total cost, and credit the account that paid. Never infer gains or prices.

## Rewards

Cashback paid into an account debits that account and credits rewards income.
A discount at the till reduces the expense.

Use the declared points account and its code. Use whole points in decimal text
such as 500.00. Debit points and credit `Income:Rewards` for earnings. Include
both postings in the purchase that earned the points. For expiry, credit points
and debit `Expenses:Points Expired` in points. Earnings and expiry have no cost.

For redemption, credit the points spent with a total cost in money, and debit
the expense with that money amount. Ask for the money value if it is missing.

## Pending entries

If the person cannot settle the category yet, use an uncategorised expense
account and mark the entry pending so it reaches Review and counts in balances.

## Unpaid bills

An unpaid bill is not a payment. Record the expense only after payment.

## Fuel log

Record litres and trip distance separately from the monetary cost, when provided.
